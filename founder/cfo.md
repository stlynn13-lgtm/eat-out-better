# CFO's note (read this first)

- **The margin:** each $29.99 founding annual leaves **$20.69** (69%) after Apple's 15%, the payer's own scans, and the free users each payer carries. At the growth plan's volume, the margin is 65%. Because per-unit costs are tiny, the margin is not the problem.
- **The line to watch is the ramp, not the cost.** The ramp assumes 2 annual sales a day from January, which is about 60 payers a month, or about 1,500 installs a month at 4% paying. The buyer panel converted **0 of 20**. At 25% of the planned volume, year 1 profit falls from $22,033 to **$2,248** and the startup money is not earned back within the year (what-if `--volume 0.25`).
- **Cash:** about **$3,062** before it pays for itself, which is the $2,700 pre-launch test budget plus one month of fixed costs. That is small. The real cost is Sean's time.
- **Break-even:** fixed costs of $362 a month ÷ $20.69 = **about 18 annual subscriptions a month** (the tool rounds this to "1 a day"). Without the $300 a month of Apple Ads it is 3 a month.
- **Three ways to improve the margin (from the tool):**
  1. Price $39.99 instead of $29.99: year 1 profit goes from $22,033 to $34,783 (+$12,750). The panel says $39.99 is above the acceptable range, though.
  2. Cut Apple Ads until cost per payer is under $20: fixed costs drop from $362 to $62 a month, and break-even drops from about 18 to about 3 subscriptions a month.
  3. Make sure the Small Business Program is in place. At a 30% commission, contribution falls by $4.50 per sale.
- Year 1 is cash basis: an annual sale counts once. Renewals (35% in the base case of `pricing-strategy.md`) are not in year 1.
- An accountant should check the LLC's tax treatment before money moves. This is not financial advice.
