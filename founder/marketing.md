## What the panel says marketing has to fix

No segment bought, so there is no "best audience" to point at yet. What does exist is a clear ranking of objections. Marketing alone can't fix any of these. Each one needs a matching product or proof point:

| Objection (count) | What answers it | Product or proof needed |
|---|---|---|
| "I already know what to order" (7) | Show the hidden butter in "grilled" fish | Chain pages: "the grilled salmon at [chain] is cooked in butter" |
| "Is this from a doctor or dietitian?" (5) | A named dietitian on every screen | Sign the advisor (growth-plan: by 30 Oct) |
| "ChatGPT does this free" (4) | A side-by-side on 15 real menus | Run the blind head-to-head and publish it |
| "Not scanning in front of people" (2+, mentioned by 6) | Check the menu at home, before you go | **Scan from a menu link or PDF (not in the backlog yet)** |

## Positioning

1. For people whose doctor told them to cut saturated fat, Eat Out Better is the menu check that finds the butter and cream hiding in "healthy" restaurant dishes, unlike a chatbot, which guesses differently every time.
2. For people managing cholesterol, Eat Out Better is the dietitian-checked restaurant guide you look at before you go, unlike generic calorie scanners.
3. For caregivers, it's the way to choose a restaurant your partner can safely enjoy.

**Recommended: #1.** It answers the largest objection group (habit, 7 of 20) and the ChatGPT objection (4), and it's the only claim no competitor makes. Panel evidence: P005 and P009 both said that being shown a hidden ingredient in their usual order would flip them.

## Ten hooks (each answers one panel objection)

1. "Your 'healthy' grilled salmon is probably cooked in butter." (habit)
2. "I checked the Olive Garden menu for high cholesterol. 3 dishes made it." (habit)
3. "A registered dietitian checked every rating in this app." (trust; only once the advisor is signed)
4. "I asked ChatGPT and this app the same menu. Here's what ChatGPT missed." (ChatGPT; only after the head-to-head)
5. "Check the menu before you leave the house. Nobody at the table has to know." (social)
6. "Your doctor said cut saturated fat. Nobody told you how to do that at Chili's." (need)
7. "Free: the 3 best picks on any menu. No trial needed." (subscription aversion)
8. "Dressing on the side isn't the problem. The cream in the soup is." (habit)
9. "Ordering for someone with high cholesterol? Pick the restaurant together." (caregiver)
10. "Statins help. What you order still counts." (need, "my statin handles it")

Rules: no health-condition targeting in ad copy ("Do you have high cholesterol?" is out on Meta), no panel quotes used as testimonials, and no "doctor-approved" until a doctor has actually approved it.
