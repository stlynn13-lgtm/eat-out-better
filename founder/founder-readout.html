Eat Out Better: pricing, competitors and go-to-market

Six founder skills run in order: competitors, buyer panel, CFO, pricing, marketing, launch. Source files are in founder/ in the repo. The buyers are simulated, so read their answers as a list of objections, not as a demand forecast.

The verdict

The per-sale margin is fine. Demand is unproven.

  1. 0 of 20 simulated buyers paid. Language models lean agreeable, so a 0% here is a loud signal, not noise. Only 2 of the 20 cited price.
  2. They pass on habit, trust and the free chatbot. "I already know what to order", "Is a dietitian behind this?" and "ChatGPT does it free" make up 16 of the 20 no's.
  3. A new finding: nobody wants to scan at the table. Six buyers said so unprompted. "Check the menu before you go" may be the real use case. Now backlog #21; the v0 (tell people screenshots work) costs nothing.
  4. No price recommendation yet. The panel's price range was withdrawn: buyers answered the price questions right after reading "$39.99 a year", so the range measured that price back. Re-run the price questions on a pitch with no price before choosing what to test in November.
  5. The plan already in growth-plan.md still holds. The repeat-use test (Gate 1, Fri 13 Nov) remains the decision point. These results add two questions for the exit calls.

1 · founder-competitors

Lots of apps, no leader, and nobody built for cholesterol

At least nine AI menu scanners launched in 2025–26, and nearly all have only a handful of ratings. Every one is either generic ("healthy", macros, seed oils) or built for allergies. None is built around saturated fat and LDL with a reason for each dish. Annual prices run from $19.99 to $39.99, with a median of about $29.99.

The gap

A clinical rule for one condition, with an explanation and a free answer. Competitors' customers complain about paywalls right after onboarding and about falsely precise numbers on mixed dishes.

The threat

MenuFit or Menu Me could add a "cholesterol" profile in a week. The protection is owning "cholesterol" in search and on clinicians' recommendation lists first.

3 · founder-pricing

No price recommendation yet

Why the price range was withdrawn

Each simulated buyer read the pitch, including "$39.99 a year" and the $29.99 launch offer, and was then asked the four price questions. Van Westendorp only works if the buyer hasn't been told a price. Half the panel gave the identical answers $5 / $15 / $30 / $40–45, round numbers around $39.99, so the range mostly measured the price it was supposed to test. The earlier "$5 to $35, test $24.99 against $34.99" came from that.

The tools are fixed: the price questions are now a separate brief, built from a pitch with every price taken out, and the pricing tool labels answers from a panel that saw a price as anchored.

Before the November pre-order test: re-run the price questions with a price-free pitch, then choose the two test prices from that, the competitor prices above, and the margins below.

What to watch: the plan assumes about 60 payers a month from January, which needs roughly 1,500 installs a month. At a quarter of that volume, year 1 profit is about $2,200 and the startup spend is not earned back within the year. You need about $3,100 in cash before the business pays for itself. The quickest gain: pause Apple Ads until the cost per payer is under $20. That drops break-even from about 18 to about 3 subscriptions a month.

5 · founder-marketing

Lead with the hidden butter

Positioning: For people whose doctor told them to cut saturated fat, Eat Out Better is the menu check that finds the butter and cream hiding in "healthy" restaurant dishes. A chatbot guesses differently every time.

*Mentioned by 6 buyers; it was the primary reason for 2.

Three channels

  • Dietitian and cardiology practices (QR cards)
  • "What to order at [chain]" search pages
  • Short video: "I checked the [chain] menu"

Not doing

  • LinkedIn to reach users
  • Product Hunt as a growth engine
  • Meta conversion ads (health ad rules)
  • Paid ads above $20 per payer

Hooks to lead with

  • "Your 'healthy' grilled salmon is probably cooked in butter."
  • "I checked the Olive Garden menu for high cholesterol. 3 dishes made it."
  • "Check the menu before you leave the house. Nobody at the table has to know."
  • "Statins help. What you order still counts."
