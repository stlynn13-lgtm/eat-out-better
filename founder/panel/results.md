## What would flip a no

- If my doctor told me my numbers were still high despite the statin and I needed to change how I eat out, and the app's ratings matched what my doctor and dietitian would say.
- If I could scan the menu at home from the restaurant's website or a PDF, without anyone seeing me do it, and it showed actual calories and saturated fat per dish.
- Proof that its ratings match real restaurant recipes or lab-tested nutrition data, for example a published accuracy check against dishes that were actually measured.
- If I could scan the menu at home before going out and show the top picks on my phone at the table, without taking a photo in the restaurant.
- If it showed me that the grilled fish I always order is actually cooked in butter or cream, something I had no idea about, and I could fix it with one swap.
- If I could get the answer without taking out my phone at the table, and it were clearly better than what I get from asking ChatGPT for free.
- A named clinician or registered dietitian standing behind the ratings, plus a plan that does not auto-renew after the trial.
- My dietitian or doctor recommends it, or the free scans turn out to match what she tells me about specific dishes.
- If it showed me that a grilled dish I order every time, like the salmon, actually comes with something on it that I didn't expect, I'd pay for that.
- If it showed estimated cholesterol-relevant numbers per dish, like saturated fat or LDL-related grams, that I could log next to my tracker.
- A clear statement that the advice is reviewed by a doctor or registered dietitian, plus a way to use it without taking my phone out at the table.
- Proof that it's clearly more accurate than a general AI assistant on real restaurant menus, for example a side-by-side test showing it catches cream sauces and frying that ChatGPT misses.
